To find the employees who earn more than their managers, 
you can use a self-join on the Employee table. 
The idea is to join the table with itself based on the managerId and id columns, 
and then filter the results to find employees whose salary is greater than their 
manager's salary.

Explanation
SELECT Clause:
Select the name column from the first instance of the Employee table (e1) 
and alias it as Employee.

FROM Clause:
Specify the Employee table with an alias e1.

JOIN Clause:
Perform a self-join with another instance of the Employee table (e2).
Join the tables on the condition that the managerId of e1 matches the id of e2.

WHERE Clause:
Filter the results to include only those rows where the salary of e1 is 
greater than the salary of e2.

Example Usage
Given the example table:

Employee Table:
+----+-------+--------+-----------+
| id | name  | salary | managerId |
+----+-------+--------+-----------+
| 1  | Joe   | 70000  | 3         |
| 2  | Henry | 80000  | 4         |
| 3  | Sam   | 60000  | Null      |
| 4  | Max   | 90000  | Null      |
+----+-------+--------+-----------+

Output:

+----------+
| Employee |
+----------+
| Joe      |
+----------+

Explanation of the Output
Joe (id = 1) has a manager with id = 3 (Sam).
Joe's salary (70000) is greater than Sam's salary (60000).
Therefore, Joe is included in the result.
Summary
This query efficiently finds employees who earn more than their managers 
by using a self-join on the Employee table and filtering based on the salary comparison. 
The result is returned in any order, as specified in the problem statement.
